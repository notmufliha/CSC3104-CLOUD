# ScamGuard usability test with seniors

A 20-minute session per person, for 3–5 seniors (65+) in Singapore. It checks that the elderly-friendly version of
ScamGuard (Android or iOS) can be set up with a family helper and used without help. Run each session one to one.
The app is in English only, so recruit participants who read English comfortably (note it if someone needed help
with the words rather than the app).

## What you need

- A test phone with ScamGuard freshly installed (debug build), volume up, ring/silent switch on silent (the red
  warning should still be heard).
- A second person to act as the family helper (a team member, or the participant's own family member if they came
  with one). Their phone number is entered as the trusted contact; the test only opens the dialler and Messages,
  nothing is called or sent.
- This sheet, a stopwatch (phone timer is fine) and the consent form below.
- No real phone calls are used: every call in ScamGuard is a pretend (practice) call.

## Consent (read aloud, then ask for a signature or a clear "yes")

> "We are students from CSC3104 Group 15. We are testing a practice app that warns people about scam calls. We are
> testing the app, not you; there are no wrong answers. It takes about 20 minutes. We will write down what happens and
> how long things take, but we will not record your voice or your face, and we will not use your name. You can stop
> at any time, and you don't need to give a reason. Is it all right to begin?"

Participant number: ______ (no names) Signature / verbal yes: ______ Date: ______

## Before the tasks (2 minutes)

Ask, and note the answers:

1. Age group: 65–69 / 70–74 / 75–79 / 80+
2. Phone you use every day: Android / iPhone / other
3. Do you use larger text on your phone? yes / no / don't know
4. Have you, or someone you know, had a scam call? (yes / no — don't ask for details)

## Tasks (about 15 minutes)

Read each task exactly as written. Don't point at the screen or name buttons. If the participant asks for help, say
"What would you try?" once; if they are still stuck after 1 minute, help and mark the task "assisted".

| # | Task (read aloud) | Success means | Time limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | (To the helper, on a freshly installed phone:) "Please set up this phone for your family member. Choose a comfortable text size, and add yourself as the family contact. You can add a PIN if you like. Then give them the phone." | On "Who will use this phone?" the helper picks **An older family member**, sets the text size, adds the contact (PIN optional) and reaches "Hand the phone to [name]"; the app ends in Simple mode | 4 min |
| 2 | (To the participant:) "Please try a practice call." | Participant starts a practice call from Home and answers it | 2 min |
| 3 | "Listen to the call. If something worries you, do what you think is right." | When the red warning appears, the participant taps **Hang up** (or **Call my family** / **Call 1799**) within 15 s of the warning | 3 min |
| 4 | "You want to tell your family about the call. Show me how." | Taps **Tell [name]** (or **Call my family**) on the warning; Messages / the dialler opens with the message or number ready | 1 min |
| 5 | "Find the calls you had today." | Finds "Recent calls" on Home (or the For family tab) and says what the last call was (e.g. "Scam") | 2 min |
| 6 | "Make the writing on the screen bigger." | Settings → Text size → Extra large | 2 min |
| 7 | (To the helper, after the session, on a second freshly installed phone or after Settings → Help → Run setup again:) "Now imagine this is your own phone. Set ScamGuard up for yourself, the way you would normally use it." | Picks **Me** on "Who will use this phone?", finishes the setup (or Skip) and lands on the full app (charts, call history, all settings); note whether "Me, but simpler" or "An older family member" confused them | 3 min |

Observe during tasks 1 and 7, without prompting:

- Did the helper read all three choices on "Who will use this phone?" before picking? Did they hesitate between
  "Me, but simpler" and "An older family member"?
- Did the helper understand they should hand the phone over at "Hand the phone to [name]"?

Observe during task 3, without prompting:

- Did they notice the warning straight away? Did they hear it read aloud?
- Did they read the message, or act on the colour / sound alone?
- Did anything scare or upset them? (Note their words.)

## What to measure

For each task:

- **Success**: done alone / done with help ("assisted") / not done.
- **Time**: from the end of reading the task to success (seconds).
- **Help requests**: how many times they asked for help or looked to you for help.
- **Errors**: wrong buttons tapped, going back, closing the warning instead of hanging up, etc.

After all tasks, ask: **"How sure do you feel that you could use this app on your own?"** and show a 1–5 card:
1 = not sure at all, 2 = a little, 3 = somewhat, 4 = quite sure, 5 = very sure.

Then ask two open questions and write down the answers word for word:

1. "What was the hardest part?"
2. "Was anything on the screen hard to read or confusing?"
